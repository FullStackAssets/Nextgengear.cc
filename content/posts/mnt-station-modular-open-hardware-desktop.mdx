On August 20, 2026 MNT Research launched the MNT Station on CrowdSupply, a modular, open‑hardware desktop that can also serve as a low‑power server or edge device. The chassis supports VESA mounting, battery backup, and a range of ARM‑based SoCs, letting users build a stationary machine without the bulk of a laptop. By exposing the mainboard and offering a flexible enclosure, the Station aims to bridge the gap between hobbyist tinkering and production‑grade hardware. Its design targets homelab enthusiasts, developers, and anyone needing a quiet, space‑saving compute node. The campaign runs until September 25, 2026, inviting backers to bring the concept to market.

## What happened
MNT Research opened the campaign on CrowdSupply on August 20, 2026, presenting the MNT Station as a quiet, space‑saving desktop that can be wall‑mounted via standard VESA or custom keyhole slots. The enclosure is designed to house the MNT Reform mainboard or any compatible ARM‑based SoC, including options from NXP, Amlogic, Qualcomm, Broadcom, and even AMD/Xilinx FPGA modules.

The device includes built‑in battery backup powered by eight LiFePO4 cells, providing several hours of runtime during power outages. It also features external antenna mounts for strong Wi‑Fi or SDR reception, a USB‑C power‑delivery port, and a silent cooling design that eliminates the need for active fans.

## Why it matters
By offering a fully open‑source hardware platform, the MNT Station empowers developers to customize their compute environment without vendor lock‑in. Its modularity means a single chassis can evolve from a desktop PC to a NAS, streaming box, or edge router, extending the lifespan of existing MNT Reform mainboards. This flexibility reduces e‑waste and lowers total cost of ownership for homelab setups, while the battery backup adds resilience for critical services.

## How to think about it
If you already own an MNT Reform mainboard, the Station is a cost‑effective way to repurpose it as a stationary device. For new builds, evaluate the SoC options that fit your workload—choose a low‑power ARM chip for a home NAS or a more capable processor for edge AI tasks. Plan your mounting location early; the VESA holes simplify integration into existing monitor or wall setups. Finally, factor in the battery module cost and ensure you have a reliable power‑delivery source for continuous operation.
