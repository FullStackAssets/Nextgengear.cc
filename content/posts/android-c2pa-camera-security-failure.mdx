The promise of C2PA‑enabled cameras was to let Android users prove that a photo came straight from the sensor, unaltered by software. Recent analysis shows that the underlying Key Attestation and Play Integrity checks can be bypassed with readily available root exploits. Attackers can now sign arbitrary files, effectively forging C2PA credentials on fully patched Pixel phones. This collapse of the trust model threatens journalism, legal evidence, and any workflow that relies on cryptographic provenance. Understanding the flaw is essential for anyone deploying C2PA in critical pipelines.

<Callout type="takeaway">C2PA signatures on Android cameras can be forged by exploiting root vulnerabilities, rendering the provenance claim unreliable.</Callout>

## What happened
C2PA camera apps on Android rely on Key Attestation and Google Play Integrity to bind the signing key to the device’s image sensor, preventing arbitrary file signing. 

Root privilege escalation exploits—including low‑cost hardware fault injection attacks and the CVE‑2026‑43499 one‑click exploit for fully patched Pixel devices—break both Key Attestation and Play Integrity, allowing attackers to sign any file as if it originated from the camera. 

These vulnerabilities stem from hardware flaws that cannot be patched on many existing devices, and the issue has been reported for over 90 days, indicating a systemic weakness rather than a fleeting zero‑day.

## Why it matters
When C2PA signatures can be forged, the core promise of cryptographic provenance evaporates, jeopardizing newsrooms, courts, and any organization that treats signed images as immutable evidence. The breach also undermines user confidence in Android’s security guarantees and pressures the broader industry to rethink attestation models.

## How to think about it
Treat Android C2PA metadata as a helpful hint, not definitive proof. Combine it with independent verification such as sensor‑level hashes, secure enclave attestation, or third‑party provenance services. Keep devices fully updated, limit root access, and consider alternative platforms for high‑stakes image authentication.

<Callout type="warning">Do not rely on Android C2PA metadata as sole evidence of authenticity; verify with independent methods.</Callout>
