The author recently migrated his first smartphone from Android to Ubuntu Touch, completing the switch in under an hour. He expected a quick weekend project and was surprised by how fast the core system installed. The experience sparked a broader reflection on why the mobile ecosystem needs a true third option beyond Android and iOS. As control over apps tightens, the stakes for user freedom grow.

<Callout type="takeaway">Ubuntu Touch proves a viable third mobile OS is essential for user freedom.</Callout>

## What happened
The author migrated his first phone from Android to Ubuntu Touch, finishing the setup in less than an hour despite expecting a longer process. He notes that while de‑googled ROMs like LineageOS or GrapheneOS offer some relief, they still sit within Google’s ecosystem and cannot fully escape its permissions model. Recent trends show Google tightening control over app distribution, making it harder for users to install software without paying fees or waiting days for approval. This environment motivated the author to test Ubuntu Touch as a genuinely independent mobile platform.

## Why it matters
A truly independent mobile OS restores user agency, allowing individuals to choose where their data goes and which software runs on their device. It also creates space for developers who want to target platforms without corporate gatekeepers, fostering competition that can improve security and innovation. Without a viable third option, the market remains duopolistic, limiting privacy choices for billions of users.

## How to think about it
Start by assessing which apps you rely on daily; if they are available through open‑source alternatives or the Waydroid container, Ubuntu Touch may meet your needs. Choose a device on the official supported list, flash the OS, and keep a backup Android installation for fallback. Treat the transition as a trial period: evaluate battery life, peripheral compatibility, and software stability before making it your primary phone.

<Callout type="warning">Expect missing mainstream apps and occasional driver quirks; plan for a secondary device if you rely on specific software.</Callout>
