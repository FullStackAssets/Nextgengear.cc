I recently ran a side‑by‑side test of three USB‑C cables using the free WhatCable utility on my MacBook. The app reads the e‑marker data from each cable, letting me verify advertised power ratings such as 240 W versus the 60 W ceiling of unmarked cables. I paired the cables with my Google Pixel 8 and a 65 W Yesido charger to see how the phone actually draws power. The results highlight why a simple software check can prevent slow charging and protect battery health.

<Callout type="takeaway">WhatCable instantly reveals whether a USB‑C cable lives up to its claimed wattage, saving you from under‑performing accessories.</Callout>

## What happened
Using a 2023 MacBook with two USB‑C ports, I installed the free WhatCable app (a 21.5 MB zip that expands to 82.2 MB). The software reads the electronic marker (e‑marker) inside each cable, reporting its maximum power rating and data speed capabilities without any external hardware.

I tested three cables: a 240 W advertised cable, a 65 W cable, and a standard USB‑2.0 charging cable lacking an e‑marker. All were connected to a Google Pixel 8 while the phone was powered by a 65 W Yesido charger. The Pixel drew a maximum of 27 W, confirming that the charger’s capacity exceeded the phone’s demand.

The 240 W cable’s e‑marker correctly reported a 240 W rating, while the 65 W cable showed 65 W. The unmarked cable was capped at 60 W, as expected for cables without an e‑marker.

## Why it matters
Android users often assume any USB‑C cable will deliver fast charging, but a cable’s internal rating determines how much power can actually flow. Using a low‑rated cable can limit charging speed, increase heat, and potentially shorten battery lifespan. By confirming a cable’s rating before purchase, consumers avoid wasted money on over‑priced cables that offer no real benefit.

## How to think about it
1. Verify that a cable’s e‑marker rating meets or exceeds the wattage of your charger.
2. Prefer cables with an e‑marker if you need more than 60 W or USB 3 data speeds.
3. Use WhatCable (or a similar tool) to confirm the rating before relying on a cable for daily charging.
4. Keep a mix of high‑rated cables for fast charging and lower‑rated, inexpensive cables for low‑power accessories.

<Callout type="warning">Downloading WhatCable from outside the Mac App Store triggers a security warning; you must allow apps from unidentified developers in System Settings → Privacy & Security before the app will run.</Callout>
